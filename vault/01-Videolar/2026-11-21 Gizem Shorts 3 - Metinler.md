North Sentinel Island, in India's Andaman Islands, is home to the Sentinelese, one of the last uncontacted peoples on Earth. ## 2) Snake Island (Ilha da Queimada Grande) — `video/out/gizem-snakeisland-en.mp4` (? s)
**Başlık:** Snake Island: Brazil's Island Where Landing Is Banned #shorts

**Açıklama:**
```
Ilha da Queimada Grande lies about 33 km off the coast of São Paulo state, Brazil. It is the only place in the world where the golden lancehead (Bothrops insularis), a highly venomous pit viper, lives. A 2008 study estimated roughly 2,000–4,000 snakes on an island of about 43 hectares; the popular "one snake per square metre" claim is an exaggeration. The island was cut off from the mainland when sea levels rose after the last ice age, and with no prey on the ground, the snakes hunt birds. A lighthouse has stood there since 1909 and has long been automatic; the story that its last keeper's family was killed by snakes is a legend with no evidence. Today only the Brazilian Navy and authorised researchers may land. The golden lancehead is classified as Critically Endangered.

Sources: Wikipedia (Ilha da Queimada Grande, Golden lancehead); Smithsonian Magazine; Discover Magazine; Martins et al. (2008) population estimate; IUCN Red List.

"Metro-2" is the informal name for a secret underground railway that is reportedly hidden beneath Moscow, said to link the Kremlin with government bunkers and other strategic sites. **Başlık:** He Knocked Down a Wall and Found an Underground City #shorts

**Açıklama:**
```
In 1963, a resident of Derinkuyu in Cappadocia, Türkiye, broke through a wall while renovating his house and found a passage. It led to a vast underground city carved into the soft volcanic rock. Derinkuyu reaches about 85 metres deep, with up to 18 levels by some counts (only part of it is open to visitors), and it may have sheltered as many as 20,000 people together with their livestock and food. It had stables, wine and oil presses, wells, chapels and ventilation shafts, and huge rolling stone doors (around half a tonne) that could seal each level from the inside. It was greatly expanded in the Byzantine era as a refuge from raids; its earliest phases may be much older. Figures are estimates and vary between sources.

## 5) Flannan Isles Lighthouse — `video/out/gizem-flannan-en.mp4` (? s)
**Başlık:** Three Lighthouse Keepers Vanished: The Flannan Isles Mystery #shorts

**Açıklama:**
```
In December 1900, the three keepers of the lighthouse on Eilean Mòr in the Flannan Isles, about 32 km west of Lewis in Scotland's Outer Hebrides, disappeared: James Ducat, Thomas Marshall and Donald MacArthur. On 15 December, the passing steamer Archtor noted the light was not lit. Storms delayed the relief vessel Hesperus until 26 December (Boxing Day), and when relief keeper Joseph Moore went ashore, the station was empty; one set of oilskins was still inside. Popular versions later added an untouched meal, an overturned chair and eerie logbook entries — the meal and chair come from Wilfrid Wilson Gibson's 1912 poem "Flannan Isle", and the logbook entries are a later invention; none appear in the official records. The Northern Lighthouse Board's superintendent, Robert Muirhead, found storm damage high above the west landing and concluded the men were most likely swept away by an unusually large wave. Their bodies were never found.

Sources: National Records of Scotland (Northern Lighthouse Board records); Wikipedia (Flannan Isles Lighthouse); Royal Museums Greenwich; History Hit.

## 6) Mary Celeste — `video/out/gizem-maryceleste-en.mp4` (? s)
**Başlık:** The Ghost Ship Found With No One On Board: Mary Celeste #shorts

**Açıklama:**
```
On 4 December 1872, the crew of the British brigantine Dei Gratia found the American brigantine Mary Celeste drifting about 400 miles east of the Azores. She had sailed from New York for Genoa with 1,701 barrels of alcohol. The ship was seaworthy and still had about six months of food and water, but the lifeboat was missing — and so were all ten people aboard: Captain Benjamin Briggs, his wife Sarah, their two-year-old daughter Sophia and seven crew. A salvage inquiry in Gibraltar found no evidence of foul play. In 1884, a young Arthur Conan Doyle published a fictional story, "J. Habakuk Jephson's Statement", whose invented details became part of the legend. One leading theory: fumes or leaking alcohol made the captain fear an explosion, everyone took to the lifeboat, and they lost the ship. The painting shows the ship in 1861, when she was still called Amazon.
