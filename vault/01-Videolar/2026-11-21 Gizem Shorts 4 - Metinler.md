## 1) Antikythera — `video/out/gizem-antikythera-en.mp4`
**Başlık:** This 2,000-Year-Old Computer Was Found in a Shipwreck #shorts

**Açıklama:**
```
In 1901, sponge divers pulled a lump of corroded bronze from a Greek shipwreck near Antikythera. Inside were gears. The Antikythera mechanism (c. 2nd–1st century BC) could show the Sun, the Moon and its phases, predict eclipses and track the four-year cycle of the Olympic Games. X-ray CT scans in 2005 revealed hidden gears and thousands of tiny inscriptions, and in 2021 a UCL team published a new model of its lost front gearing. Who built it is still unknown.

Sources: UCL Antikythera Research Team, Freeth et al., Scientific Reports 11, 5821 (2021); Alexander Jones (ISAW/NYU); National Archaeological Museum, Athens.

## 2) Baalbek — `video/out/gizem-baalbek-en.mp4`
**Başlık:** The Biggest Stone Block of the Ancient World: Baalbek #shorts

**Açıklama:**
```
In a Roman quarry at Baalbek, Lebanon, lie the largest stone blocks known from antiquity. The Stone of the Pregnant Woman weighs about 1,000 tonnes, and in 2014 the German Archaeological Institute found an even larger block beside it: about 19.6 m long and roughly 1,650 tonnes. The temple's base, the trilithon, holds three blocks of about 800 tonnes each. Most archaeologists credit Roman engineering (ramps, rollers, levers and capstans from a quarry slightly uphill, about 800 m away); exactly how the trilithon was lifted is still debated. Weights are estimates and vary between sources.

Sources: German Archaeological Institute (DAI) via Archaeology magazine (Dec 2014; Mar/Apr 2015) and Smithsonian; Guinness World Records, "Largest megalith from antiquity".
